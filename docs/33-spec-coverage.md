# 33 — Spec Coverage: what is built, what is not, and what is unproven

> A scorecard against all 85 sections of `SPEC-master-prompt.md`, written at
> the end of Phase 4. Its job is to be **checkable**: every "built" names where
> the behaviour lives and what proves it, and every gap says why it is a gap
> rather than an oversight.
>
> Three states are used, and the third matters most:
>
> - **Built** — implemented, tested, and used through the UI at least once.
> - **Deliberately not built** — a decision, with the reason. Not a to-do.
> - **Unproven** — code exists; the claim the spec makes cannot be verified
>   here. These are the ones that would embarrass us in an incident, so they
>   are named individually rather than averaged away.

| 23  | Growth journey as its own ladder                    | It _is_ the rank engine with different words (docs/27 §3). Two ladders would disagree about what a member achieved.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

Would need enough CRM history to be worth trusting.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| 41  | Business OS as a distinct module                    | Its listed capabilities are CRM + commerce + analytics, already present.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| 48  | The full AI agent roster                            | One assistant and one coach exist. Agents without jobs are ceremony.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 77  | An app directory / third-party publishing           | A marketplace of integrations is a directory, and there are no third-party integrations to list — an empty room with a sign on it. What integrators actually lacked was a machine-readable description of the API, which docs/47 builds.                                                                                                                   |
| 62  | Storage (R2/S3 uploads)                             | Nothing in the shipped surface uploads a file yet.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |

Moving money needs a provider and a licence conversation, not code.                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

## 3. Unproven — code exists, the claim does not hold yet

These are the honest risks. Each names what would make it provable.

| Claim                                                                                                   | State                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | What it needs                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A large tenant can move to a dedicated database with zero data loss** (§61, ADR-002, roadmap Phase 4) | Routing seam, extract/verify tooling and runbook (docs/31–32). Rehearsed at 340k rows 2026-08-21: 87/87 tables matched, 10.3s read-only window (docs/40 §5).                                                                                        | The rehearsal runs on scratch copies of a laptop database. A staging rehearsal with a real tenant's data, and a measured window on production-grade hardware.                      |
| **Enterprise SSO works with a real IdP**                                                                | OIDC verified against a fake provider in tests.                                                                                                                                                                                                     | One federated tenant against Entra/Okta/Google, including their clock skew and claim quirks.                                                                                       |
| **The outbox survives production load**                                                                 | Measured: 125 events/s with handlers after Sprint 24's per-event transactions (157 before), 10/s before Sprint 22 removed a throttle. One slow handler now delays one event, not twenty (docs/41, docs/43).                                         | A real production load profile. The remaining upgrade is BullMQ (docs/11), which is a scaling decision now rather than a rescue.                                                   |
| **Scheduled work happens**                                                                              | Renewals, rank evaluation, commission drafts, the webhook sweep and now the alert sweep run on a timer, one row per occurrence (docs/35). Runs left `claimed` are alerted on, not merely displayed (docs/42).                                       | A second API instance to prove the claim across processes in a real deployment. The mechanism is proved in `second-instance.e2e.spec.ts`; the operational setup is not.            |
| **AI answers are grounded**                                                                             | Citations and a grounded-local fallback; quota-capped.                                                                                                                                                                                              | A real provider key, and evaluation against questions with known answers.                                                                                                          |
| **Rate limiting holds**                                                                                 | All five tiers docs/10 §8 defines are enforced: public API by key (docs/30), pre-auth by attempt (docs/48), and read/write/expensive/tenant-global by `(tenant, user)` (docs/49). One budget in Redis across instances, in-memory fallback.         | A deployment that actually runs two instances. Every tier is proved in test; none has met production traffic.                                                                      |
| **Backups restore**                                                                                     | `scripts/restore-drill.sh` dumps, restores into a scratch database and verifies structure, every populated table's row count, policies, grants and a live cross-tenant isolation probe. Passed on the dev database 2026-08-21 (docs/39 §5).         | The same drill against a staging restore of **production** backups. What is proved today is the procedure, not the provider's backups.                                             |
| **Defence in depth holds on every path**                                                                | True for tenant-scoped routes, and now for cross-tenant platform READS: `aviora_platform` is policy-bound on all 89 tenant-isolated tables and sees nothing unless a transaction declares itself (docs/53). Migrations and seeding stay owner work. | A platform read that declares itself still crosses tenants — that is its job. What changed is that the exemption is stated per transaction by a role that cannot alter the schema. |
| **PII is encrypted at rest where it matters**                                                           | Health free text and the OIDC client secret are AES-256-GCM, fail-closed. CRM lead/customer name, email and phone are **plaintext** — the security doc claimed otherwise until this was audited (docs/13 §11.1).                                    | A decision from whoever owns key custody: encrypting existing rows is one-way, and it should be all three columns or none.                                                         |
| **Somebody is watching**                                                                                | Five checks over the numbers docs/36 computes, swept every 5 minutes as a scheduler job, emailing on change rather than on every sweep (docs/42). Every answer carries when the checks last ran.                                                    | A second channel, and somebody actually on call. One email address is one point of failure, and an alert nobody is rota'd to read is a page nobody opens.                          |

## 4. The MVP rows

`docs/14` §3 row 16 — the AI respecting _team-level_ knowledge permissions —
was the last one open, and closed in Sprint 18 (docs/37). Knowledge can now be
attached to a team; reading it goes up the tree and publishing it goes down;
and the assistant respects the boundary because retrieval never loads an
article the caller may not read, so there is nothing to filter afterwards.

Row 18 (mobile UX) stays ⚠️ manual on purpose. Playwright runs at Pixel 7 and
asserts no horizontal scroll, but "usable" is a judgement a viewport check does
not make, and turning that row green on the strength of a scroll-width
assertion would claim more than the test proves.
